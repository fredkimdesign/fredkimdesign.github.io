**The pattern:** the people already inside do not run 3,000-word process
case studies. Their sites are short, dated, opinionated, and heavy on
*things that exist* — shipped products, working prototypes, talks, writing.
Two of the most senior have sites that are literally toys. The signal they
send is: I make things, they work, I have taste, and I don't need to prove
it at length.

That is not a license to skip case studies. It is a description of the
finish line, not the qualifying round. These people are known quantities;
Fred is applying. But it sets the *voice and density* target for the site
itself: sound like someone who already belongs there.

## What the review actually probes

From the [Anthropic product designer interview guide](https://interview.norahq.com/interview-guides/anthropic-product-designer-interview-guide-2026):

- 60 minutes, one or two projects deep.
- "Visual polish matters less than reasoning, structure, and how clearly you
  explain decisions."
- Probes: problem selection, **constraints that shaped the design**,
  **alternatives considered and rejected**, **changes you'd make with more
  time or data**.
- Weak candidates "focus on visual aesthetics rather than reasoning, skip
  collaboration details, and struggle to articulate why specific directions
  were chosen over alternatives."

Those four probes map one-to-one onto the schema: `stakes.constraint`,
`bet.rejected`, `friction`. That was not designed to the guide — it was
designed to what the studies were missing — but it's confirmation the shape
is right.

From the [OpenAI Product Designer, ChatGPT posting](https://builtin.com/job/product-designer-chatgpt/4457411):
"strong UX, UI, and interaction design skills, with a high bar for quality
and craft"; "impactful communication and storytelling skills"; enjoy
"tackling ambiguous problems and shaping them into a clear vision."

From the [Anthropic Product Designer, Design Systems posting](https://www.builtinnyc.com/job/product-designer-design-systems/4832340) —
the most relevant posting to Fred's current work:

> "speed of innovation matters more than consistency"

> balance "system consistency with speed of innovation"

> "Create and evolve AI Primitives — the key patterns native to AI products"

> "Contribute to design guidelines, documentation, and frameworks that help
> teams ship experiments faster"

> "a builder mindset to explore and communicate through prototyping"

**This is the Walmart friction beat, verbatim, as a job requirement.** The
experimental-layer story — a standard high enough to strand fast-moving teams,
and a tier built to fix it — is the exact tension Anthropic's design systems
role is hiring to resolve. Walmart needs to lead the site for this reason
alone.

## What the leaders say they value

Jenny Wen ([Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-design-process-is-dead), [Medium recap](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/jenny-wen-the-design-process-has-changed-7b3b586ecab0), [DesignX talk](https://designx.community/talks/jenny-wen-(design-lead-anthropic)-craft-is-counter-intuitive)):

- "It's just the ability to execute and make something good. I think shipping
  is a skill and right now, in the way that we work, people either have it or
  they don't."
- "For a given sub-feature, I'm actually just working with one engineer as
  opposed to five to 10."
- Craft is counter-intuitive: "starting in high-fidelity," "flexing
  intuition," going "deeply into the details in design crit," "changing things
  at the last minute." "They're uncomfortable, but we can't stay comfortable
  to create great craft."
- Stepped back from Director at Figma to IC at Anthropic because "the most
  alive frontier of design today is being close to the product, the code, and
  the model."

Meaghan Choi ([Product School](https://productschool.substack.com/p/anthropic-head-of-design-on-how-claude)):

- "I think of titles a little bit as a specialty you might bring to the team,
  but it doesn't delineate what you can contribute to the project."
- "We've now pushed that decision-making into live working code. You need to
  be using it yourself."
- Pods of 3–5; designers push code; quality is judged in working software,
  not in files.

Ian Silber ([Roger Wong interview](https://rogerwong.me/2026/04/designing-at-openai-ian-silber)):

- "Designers working on this are hopefully spending a lot less time in Figma
  … and more time really thinking about how you interact with this thing."
- OpenAI runs a "Dynamic User Interface Library" — primitives the model
  composes at runtime, shaped by prompt and context rather than pre-drawn
  flows.

## Implications for this site

1. **Walmart leads.** It is current, it is the hardest problem, and it is
   the literal job description for the most relevant open role.
2. **The site must itself be evidence.** Every one of these people ships.
   This portfolio is a Next.js app built with Claude Code — say so, in one
   line, somewhere visible. It is not a gimmick; it is exactly what Wen and
   Choi say they screen for.
3. **Sound like you already belong.** Short, declarative, dated. No "I
   collaborate cross-functionally." No process vocabulary. Every sentence
   specific to the project it's in.
4. **Cut to three or four studies.** Walmart, Sam's Club, and the best one or
   two of Twitter / Tesla. Plus.ai, Zoe, and NxStop don't clear the bar and
   dilute the ones that do.
5. **Add a dated "now" strip or feed** in the Jenny Wen mode — talks,
   experiments, things in flight — so the site reads as a live person rather
   than a frozen archive. Item Tile in progress belongs there.
6. **Prototype-forward, not screenshot-forward.** Wherever a case study can
   embed a working thing instead of a picture of one, do that. The tile
   across locales at worst-case string lengths should be a live component,
   not a PNG.
7. **Anthropic's own visual language** (from [type.today](https://type.today/en/journal/anthropic), [Geist](https://geist.co/work/anthropic)):
   Styrene headlines, Tiempos body, rust-orange #C15F3C on warm tan. This
   site's Newsreader + Inter + warm paper + rust accent is adjacent without
   being a costume. Keep it adjacent. Don't move closer.
